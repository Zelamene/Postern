01

The Story

Why the brand looks like a ledger and not like a startup.

Postern is built for one moment: you are asked for an email address by a service you do not entirely trust. You give it something disposable. Mail arrives somewhere you control. If it ever gets sold, you know exactly which gate it came through — and you close that gate without changing your identity.

That moment is not exciting. It is administrative. The brand should feel the way a good clerk feels: unhurried, precise, and completely unimpressed by you. So the system is built from three materials — paper, ink and sealing wax — and nothing else. Warm white paper, near-black ink, one deep maroon that behaves like a stamp: rare, decisive, and impossible to miss when it appears.

The restraint is also an accessibility strategy. A palette of one accent and four neutrals is a palette you can validate exhaustively: every foreground/background pair in this document is measured, and the measured pairings are the only ones allowed. Colour is never the only carrier of meaning, because maroon and rust sit too close together on a screen for anyone to be asked to tell them apart without a label.

i

Clarity is the feature

The product's promise is comprehension: what came in, from whom, and what happened to it. Any decoration that competes with a fact is removed.

ii

The default is quiet

No gradients, no glass, no ambient movement. Motion exists to explain a change of state in under a third of a second, and it is allowed to be switched off.

iii

Honest about the seams

Subjects are cut at 60 characters. Logs live 90 days. The backend may be asleep. We say so, in the interface, in plain words — a limitation named is a limitation trusted.

iv

One accent, earned

Maroon marks the decision and the danger. If a screen has two maroon buttons, it has no primary action. Signal hues appear only as tinted status surfaces.

v

Legible in daylight

This product is used on a phone, outdoors, by someone annoyed. 16px body, 4.5:1 minimum, 24px targets minimum, and nothing important conveyed by hue alone.

Ledger note

The name is the brief. A postern is not a backdoor — it is a designed, supervised, narrower entrance. Everything in this system should read as deliberate access rather than secrecy.

02

Logo & Iconography

An arch with a smaller door cut into it. Clear strokes and quiet symbols.

Postern

Primary mark · horizontal lockup · app tile (maroon) · inverse tile (paper). The mark is drawn on a 32 unit grid with a 3 unit stroke; the inner door is 2 units and sits right of centre, because a postern is never the main gate.

Construction & clear space

x x

x = the width of the mark's stroke gap, 2 units at 32px.

Minimum clear space is 1x on every side, and never less than the height of the arch shoulder. Text, rules and other marks stay outside it.

Minimum sizes

  • Mark: 20px digital · 8mm print
  • Lockup: 96px wide digital · 28mm print
  • App tile: 24px, no inner door below 32px
  • Favicon: use the solid tile, not the outline

Below the minimum, drop the inner door first, then the lockup's wordmark — never the outer arch.

Forbidden treatments

  • Stretching, squashing or rotating
  • Recolouring outside the palette (no black, no white-on-white, no signal hues)
  • Drop shadows, inner shadows, bevels, glows, gradients
  • Outlines traced over photographs or busy imagery
  • Placing on mid-tone backgrounds below 3:1 contrast
  • Rearranging the lockup or adding a tagline inside it
  • Replacing the wordmark with another typeface or with letter-spaced capitals
  • Animating the mark in a loop; the only permitted animation is the one-time draw
  • Using the mark as a bullet, an avatar, or inside body text

Iconography

Icons are sourced from the Lucide library (ISC licence) and inlined as SVG. Icons support text comprehension; they are never purely decorative, never colored in isolation, and never substitute for an accessible label.

03

Colour

Maroon is the colour of dried sealing wax and of the ledger's leather spine. It is not a brand red chosen to feel energetic; it is a mark — the thing pressed onto a document to say this is finished. That is why it is used at 11:1 against white and why it is allowed to appear on only one control per screen.

Everything else is paper or ink. Neutrals are warmed toward the maroon (every ink has a red undertone, every paper a cream one) so a purely neutral grey never appears — a cold grey next to this maroon looks like a rendering error.

The rule that keeps this honest

Maroon and rust are not interchangeable. Maroon is intent — an action you are taking. Rust is failure — something that happened to you. They measure at 10.45:1 and 7.79:1 respectively against paper, which means they are both dark enough to look similar at a glance. Every use of either must be accompanied by an icon or a word, never carried by hue alone.

  • Proportion: 60% paper, 30% ink, 10% maroon. Never invert this.
  • One accent per view: a maroon primary plus one signal tint, maximum.
  • No pure black, no pure grey: every neutral carries the warm tint.
  • No gradients or glows: elevation is a shadow token, not a colour.
  • Dark theme: the same roles at the other end of the day, with its own measured pairs — see 04 · Dark mode. Maroon on paper remains the default; nothing new is invented for it.

Ledger note

The maroon is a single hue at ten stops. Two stops are load-bearing: --maroon-700 for anything interactive, --maroon-800 for anything irreversible. If you reach for --maroon-500 to make something "pop", you are decorating. Use size, weight or space instead.

04

Dark mode

The same roles at the other end of the day: one hue, two polarities, no second stylesheet.

A mailbox is read at 23:40 as often as at 09:00, and a wall of white at that hour is a flashbang. Dark mode here is not a theme layered on top of the brand — it is the same design with paper and ink exchanged and the wax allowed to catch the light. Nothing appears in dark that does not already exist as a role in tokens.css.

Mechanically there is one definition per role, not two stylesheets: --color-bg is light-dark(#fbf8f4, #151110). One color-scheme declaration — set by data-theme on <html>, or by the operating system when that attribute is absent — switches every role at once, including native controls, scrollbars and the <dialog> backdrop. Because a role can only be declared once, the two polarities cannot drift apart.

The eight invariants

True in both polarities. The contrast ones are enforced by scripts/contrast-audit.mjs, not by memory.

  1. One brand hue. Maroon is the only accent in either polarity. Dark mode does not buy you a second accent, a glow, or a gradient.
  2. The hue is constant; only its value moves. Wax in a dark room catches light, so the accent rises to a fill at #B04554 and text/rings rise further to #E7A0AA. Both stops are measured; neither is derived by lightening until it looks right.
  3. Body text is AAA (7:1) in both themes. 15.91:1 on paper, 16.85:1 on ink. Nothing ships on a maybe, and no dark surface is allowed to sit so close to its text that AA is the ceiling.
  4. Control borders hold 3:1 non-text contrast against the surface they sit on — 3.03:1 on paper in light, 3.35:1 on card in dark. A border you can only see on a good monitor is decoration.
  5. Signals keep their hue family and their measured pair. Success is green-on-green-tint in both themes, not green-on-grey. A chip that changes family between themes stops being a signal.
  6. Elevation is surface plus hairline, never shadow alone. A dark shadow on dark ink is invisible long before the user notices the missing depth, so every raised thing also carries a 1px hairline.
  7. Dark is warm ink (#151110), never #000. Pure black kills the paper metaphor and makes maroon read as a cut-out sticker rather than a seal.
  8. Never invert by filter. No filter: invert(), no automatic recolouring of the mark, no reversed logo screenshots. The inverse mark is its own asset, drawn at the same stroke weights.

Wiring the toggle

Three states, not two: follow system, light, dark. Persist the choice and apply it before first paint, otherwise a dark-mode user gets a white flash on every navigation. 

const root = document.documentElement;
const stored = localStorage.getItem('postern-theme');
if (stored === 'light' || stored === 'dark') root.dataset.theme = stored;

function choose(next) {
  if (next === 'system') delete root.dataset.theme;
  else root.dataset.theme = next;
  next === 'system' ? localStorage.removeItem('postern-theme')
                    : localStorage.setItem('postern-theme', next);
  syncThemeColor();
}

In the document head, before any stylesheet paints, run the two lines that read localStorage and set dataset.theme. Keep <meta name="theme-color"> in step from the same function, so the mobile URL bar follows the page rather than fighting it.

  • Never branch a component on theme. If a component needs to know which polarity it is in, a role is missing from tokens.css.
  • The toggle is a segmented control, not a switch. A switch cannot express "follow system", and that is the state most people actually want.
  • No colour transition on the page. Do not animate background-color on html; the change is instant, like a light being switched.
  • Images need a dark export. A diagram exported on white paper is a white rectangle at night. Ship a dark asset or place it on a deliberate light well.
  • Scoped polarity is legal. The two panes above are subtrees with data-theme, which is why this document can show both at once without a screenshot.

Ledger note

The toast is the exception worth understanding. It stays inverse in both themes — dark ink on paper, raised slab on ink — because a toast is not a page element, it is an interruption. Same for tooltips. If you find yourself wanting a maroon toast, you want a banner.

06

Tokens

Nine groups, one file. If a value is not a token, it does not exist.

Tokens are declared in web/src/styles/tokens.css inside Tailwind's @theme, so every token is also a utility. There are two layers: primitives named for what they are (--maroon-700), and semantics named for what they do (--color-primary). Components may only read the semantic layer — that is what allows the palette to change without a component audit.

07

Space & Layout

One column on a phone, twelve on a desk, and nothing in between that surprises you.

12 columns · 24px gutter · content max 1152px (72rem) · prose max 672px (42rem) · page gutter clamp(20px, 4vw, 48px). Content never touches the viewport edge, and a line of body text never exceeds 68 characters.

Vertical rhythm

  • Inside a component: 4, 8, 12 or 16px. Never more.
  • Between components: 24px (--space-6) as the default gap.
  • Between sections: 48–96px, scaling with viewport.
  • Optical alignment: text aligns to text. Icons sit on the cap height, not the line box.
  • Touch targets: 44×44px minimum on touch, 32×32px minimum for pointer-only controls, with 24×24px as the WCAG 2.2 floor.

Responsive is a layout decision, not a font-size decision

Type already scales fluidly through clamp(), so a phone is not a shrunken desktop — it is a different composition. Below 768px the alias table becomes a stack of cards, the replace-alias checklist becomes a full-height sheet, and the side rail becomes a horizontal strip that scrolls with the content rather than pinning to the viewport.

Motion & Loading

Motion explains a change of state. It never decorates one.

The product's honest weakness is latency: a free-tier backend that sleeps, a worker that logs asynchronously, a KV write that can fail and reconcile later. Those waits are real, and pretending otherwise with a spinner is a kind of lying. So the rule is not "always show a spinner" — it is show the smallest true thing.

The loading decision

Which loading treatment to use for which wait
The waitTreatmentWhy
Under 150ms — most reads Nothing A flash of indicator is slower than the wait it describes, and it makes an instant action feel unstable.
A form submit or a row action Button spinner Keeps the label, disables the control, sets aria-busy. The user sees which action is pending, not just that something is.
150ms–3s with a known shape Skeleton Preserves layout, so the page does not jolt when content arrives. Must match the real row height exactly.
Over 3s, or unknown duration Skeleton + text Name the wait: "Waking up the backend…". Add a retry affordance after 15s. Silence at this length reads as failure.
Measurable progress — re-sync, export Determinate bar Show real counts (“2 of 5 aliases”). Never animate a fake percentage.
Route change 2px top bar Appears only after 150ms. No full-page spinner; the shell stays usable.
Copy to clipboard, toggle, optimistic edit Instant + toast Hide the latency, roll back on failure with an inline correction. Never show a spinner for something that is already done.
Nightly jobs, background sync Nothing Surface it as a count in the admin panel. The user did not ask, so they are not waiting.

The one-line rule

Skeleton when you know the shape. Spinner when you know the action. Nothing when you know neither. A skeleton is a promise about geometry — if the final content does not occupy the same box, use a spinner instead.

Skeleton anatomy

  • Same height, same column widths, same count as the loaded state — capped at six rows.
  • One sweeping highlight at 1.6s. Under prefers-reduced-motion it is a static tint.
  • Never a skeleton for a list that may be empty; show the empty state once you know.
  • Never a skeleton for longer than eight seconds; escalate to text and a retry.
  • Never skeleton a whole page when one panel is reloading. Scope the wait to what is actually waiting.

What may animate

Only opacity, transform and colour are animated — never layout properties, never anything that triggers reflow.

Permitted

  • Colour and border on hover and press (80ms)
  • Dialog scale-and-rise on open, backdrop fade (320ms)
  • Toast entering from the bottom edge (200ms)
  • Switch thumb travel with a light spring
  • Section reveal on first scroll into view (520ms, once)
  • One-time logo draw on the landing page

Forbidden

  • Parallax, autoplaying carousels, looping ambient motion
  • Anything over 520ms without a user-initiated trigger
  • Animating height, width, top or left
  • Bouncing, wobbling or attention-seeking easing
  • Motion that carries meaning with no static equivalent
  • Animating the mark in a loop, ever

Ledger note

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce collapses every animation to 0.01ms globally, from base.css. That includes the skeleton sweep, the dialog entrance and the section reveal. Nothing in this system depends on motion to be understood — which is why switching it off is safe rather than degrading.

Contrast guarantees

Every text pairing used in the product is listed in the colour plate with its measured ratio. The summary: body 15.91:1, secondary 11.69:1, muted 7.46:1, captions 5.61:1, disabled 4.84:1, primary button label 11.06:1, error text 7.79:1 — all at or above AA, most at AAA. Non-text contrast (1.4.11) is met by the control border at 3.03:1 and by the focus ring at 10.45:1. Dark mode holds the same floor with its own stops — 16.85:1 body, 3.35:1 border, 8.94:1 ring — rather than inheriting light ratios that no longer apply.

Colour is never the only signal

Every status chip carries a word. Every error carries an icon and a sentence. Every link is underlined. The one place this matters most is flag severity, where maroon and rust are 2.6:1 apart in hue but both dark reds — so "Unexpected sender" and "Failed authentication" differ by label and icon, not by shade.

Focus

On paper

On maroon

  • Always visible: 2px solid maroon with a 2px offset. Never outline: none.
  • On maroon: the ring flips to paper and keeps the offset gap, so it is visible against both the control and its background.
  • Never obscured: sticky headers carry scroll-padding-top so a focused field is never hidden behind them (2.4.11).
  • Order matches meaning: visual order and DOM order never disagree; no positive tabindex anywhere.

Forms and announcements

  • Labels are <label for>, always visible. Placeholder text is never a label.
  • Hints and errors are joined with aria-describedby; invalid fields get aria-invalid="true".
  • Submitting an invalid form moves focus to an error summary at the top of the form, listing each field as a link.
  • Toasts live in a aria-live="polite" region. Only blocking failures use role="alert".
  • Password fields accept paste, and never ask for a puzzle, a pattern or a memory test (3.3.8).
  • Loading regions set aria-busy; progress bars expose real aria-valuenow.

Reflow, zoom and motion

  • No horizontal scrolling at 320px wide (1.4.10) — the only exception is a code block.
  • Layout survives 200% text zoom and the WCAG text-spacing overrides without clipping.
  • prefers-reduced-motion removes all transitions and the skeleton sweep.
  • Nothing flashes more than three times per second; there is no auto-playing video or audio.

Voice & Tone

Like a good clerk: plain, exact, and never flustered on your behalf.

Postern writes in the second person, in the active voice, in short declarative sentences. It does not congratulate you, it does not apologise for things that are not its fault, and it never uses a metaphor where a number would do. When something is limited — 60 characters, 90 days, 500 messages, 199 seats — the number appears in the sentence.

We are

  • Plain: "Block sender" not "Suppress origin domain"
  • Specific: "Saved 90 days" not "Retained as required"
  • Calm: no exclamation marks, no capitalised alarm
  • Direct about limits: "The backend was asleep. Your mail still forwarded; the log may be missing."
  • Non-blaming: the user is never wrong, the input is

We are not

  • Cute: no "oops", no "whoops", no emoji in UI copy
  • Salesy: no "supercharge", "seamless", "effortless"
  • Vague: no "something went wrong" without a next step
  • Patronising: no "simply" or "just" — if it were simple, you would not be reading this
  • Clever: the product is the personality
